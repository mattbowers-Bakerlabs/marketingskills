# Local-Service Lead Magnet Formats (BML)

Structure and rules for the local-service types listed in SKILL.md. Every example uses placeholders (`<service>`, `<town>`, `<n>`); fill them only from the client's rows, never from this file. Rules referenced as §n are in `bml/context-rules.md`.

## Rules for every format

- **Spine:** one belief (F-8 or F-9 usually) and one proof point (G row). Without both, the footer reads `Distinctiveness: FAIL — not client-ready`.
- **Facts:** only from page 13 or cited rows. Missing fact → `[NEEDS PROOF: <row ID>]`. No invented figures, projects, timelines or quotes.
- **Voice and design:** client voice (01), layout and colours per 02 Brand Style Guide, CTA in 01's required language.
- **Guardrails:** check page 12 and F-13 at any status, plus the segment rules (§7).
- **Length:** 1–4 pages. A local buyer reads it on a phone between calls.
- **Last section:** what happens when they contact the client (D-3), and the CTA. No "book a demo".

## Cost guide — "What affects the cost of <service> in <town>"

1. Why prices vary (from D-6 buyer doubts and F-8)
2. The <n> factors that move the price (scope, materials D-10, access, permits, timing) — factors from D-2/D-3/D-10, not industry lists
3. Price ranges — **only if D-4 approves publishing them**, quoted as the row states, with "⚠ confirm before publishing". Otherwise this section explains how the client quotes, with `[NEEDS PROOF: D-4]` for the team to decide
4. Financing, packages or maintenance plans (D-7), only if on file
5. Red flags in a quote (F-9), with no competitor named (J-4)
6. Next step: how to get a quote (I-9, I-3)

Price presentation beyond a guide (a pricing page, packages) routes to **offers**.

## Planning checklist — "Before your <service> project"

- Grouped by phase, following the client's real process (D-3): decide, prepare, during, after
- Each item one action, one line; permits and licensing as J-3 requires
- Seasonal notes from I-10; service area edges from B-8
- Home services: project photos only with a release on file (J-6); never AI images presented as real projects

## "What to ask before hiring a <service> provider"

- <n> questions a buyer should ask anyone, each with "what a good answer sounds like"
- Built on D-6 (doubts), F-9 (mistakes), D-5 (guarantees), G-1 (credentials)
- The client's answers come from rows, stated plainly; avoid claims everyone makes (E-8) and anything on J-2
- Never names or implies a specific competitor (J-4)

## Seasonal maintenance guide — "<Season> <thing> checklist for <town> homeowners"

- Tasks by month or season from I-10; what a homeowner can do vs what needs a pro
- Maintenance plans or memberships only if D-7 lists them
- Promote it ahead of the season; note B-6 capacity (e.g. booked out) as context and flag it in the footer

## Patient prep guide (clinical) — "Preparing for your <procedure/visit>"

- What to bring, what to expect, how long it takes, insurance and payment basics, access (hours, parking, booking)
- Every clinical statement (preparation, risks, outcomes) comes from the client's approved published copy, and the draft is flagged for review by a named provider before use
- Reassurance, not fear: no "don't risk…", no urgency or scarcity, no outcome promises unless a page 12 row permits them
- No patient stories unless written authorization is on file (G-5, J-6); never a composite
- Gating a patient guide is a choice for the client: an ungated page often serves patients better. Lead forms and pixels on patient-facing pages are flagged for a person (§7)

## Upgrading an existing asset

If an approved `asset` record covers the topic, propose a refresh (new season, updated D-4 ranges, new G-3 story) instead of a new magnet.
